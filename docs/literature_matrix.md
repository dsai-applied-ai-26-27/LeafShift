# Literature matrix - LeafShift

Each row is one source. The last column records the decision it causes in our project.

| Source (author, year, link) | Problem addressed | Method and data | Evaluation used | Result and limitation | What we adopt or change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mohanty, Hughes, Salathé (2016). *Using deep learning for image-based plant disease detection.* Frontiers in Plant Science 7:1419. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2016.01419 | Can a deep CNN identify crop and disease from a single leaf photo? | AlexNet and GoogLeNet, trained from scratch and with ImageNet transfer learning, on PlantVillage (54,306 images, 14 crops, 26 diseases, 38 classes). Colour, grayscale and segmented versions; train-test splits from 80-20 to 20-80. The authors note that PlantVillage has several images of the same leaf and keep them in the same split (leaf mapping for 41,112 images). | Mean F1 score (primary), plus mean precision, mean recall and overall accuracy. External check on 121 and 119 images from Bing Image Search and IPM Images, reported as accuracy and top-5 accuracy. | 99.35% accuracy (mean F1 0.9934) on held-out PlantVillage (GoogLeNet, transfer learning, colour, 80-20). On the two external sets, the best models reached only 31.40% and 31.69% accuracy (top-5: 52.89% and 65.61%). On PlantVillage, transfer learning was always better and colour images worked best; on external dataset 1, the best model was the one trained on segmented images. Limitation for us: the external sets are very small and reported with accuracy only, over 38 classes instead of our 10. | **Adopt:** ImageNet transfer learning, colour images for training, and mean (macro) F1 as the primary metric. **Change:** we split PlantVillage by leaf, not at random. The authors' published leaf maps (github.com/salathegroup/plantvillage_deeplearning_paper_dataset, `leaf_grouping/filtered_leafmaps`) give a leaf ID for 11,735 of our 17,080 images (68.7%, 2,935 leaves, 4 images per leaf except one leaf with 3). We supplemented the published maps with filename-derived leaf-series IDs and conservative capture groups. Tomato mosaic virus still has only one usable capture group. We use a larger external test set of 893 cleaned PlantDoc images and report 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. |
| Singh, Jain, Jain, Kayal, Kumawat, Batra (2020). *PlantDoc: A dataset for visual plant disease detection.* CoDS-COMAD 2020, pp. 249-253. https://doi.org/10.1145/3371158.3371196 (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10317) | There was no large public dataset of plant disease images taken outside the lab. | About 20,900 images downloaded from Google Images and Ecosia and filtered by four people using APS guidelines. Every image was checked by two people, and classes with fewer than 50 images were dropped, giving 2,598 images, 13 species and 27 classes. Classification with VGG16 on whole images, and with VGG16, InceptionV3 and InceptionResNetV2 on 9,216 cropped leaves. Inputs 100x100; weights from ImageNet or ImageNet+PlantVillage. | Accuracy and F1. 80/20 split for PlantDoc training. Models trained on PlantVillage were tested on 100% of PlantDoc. | VGG16 trained on PlantVillage reached 15.08% accuracy (F1 0.15) on whole PlantDoc images, and 19.73-39.87% on cropped leaves depending on the model. With ImageNet+PlantVillage weights fine-tuned on 80% of PlantDoc, VGG16 reached 29.73% on the other 20% (13.74% with ImageNet weights only). The authors state that some images may be wrongly labelled (lack of domain expertise), that tomato bacterial spot and Septoria look similar, and that models fail on background noise, leaves from several classes and low resolution. Limitation for us: all 27 classes, 100x100 inputs. | **Adopt:** PlantDoc as our external test set, in their setting "train on PlantVillage, test on all PlantDoc images". **Change:** restrict to our 10 shared classes. Use the documented duplicate decisions and the cleaned manifest. Independently review unclear source labels when assessing label noise. Use 224x224 inputs. Report the bacterial spot vs Septoria confusion explicitly in the error analysis. |
| Noyan (2022). *Uncovering bias in the PlantVillage dataset.* arXiv:2206.04374. https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04374 | Does PlantVillage contain bias that lets a model predict the class without looking at the disease? | From each PlantVillage image, 8 background pixels (4 corners, 4 side centres). Random forest (scikit-learn, default settings), random 80/20 split. MNIST with the same 8 pixels as a control. Image blur used as a single feature on full images, foreground-only (segmented) images and background-only images. | Classification accuracy compared with random guessing. | 8 pixels gave 49.0% accuracy vs 2.6% random guessing; the MNIST control gave 11.7% vs 10%. Blur alone gave 11.7% (full), 10.0% (foreground only) and 10.8% (background only), so "background removal does not eliminate the bias": capture conditions also affect the leaf itself. The author recommends reporting performance on a separate dataset that matches the use case. Limitation for us: no deep model and no field test, so it shows that bias exists, not how much accuracy it costs in the field. | **Adopt:** Grad-CAM on both lab and field images, to show whether the model looks at the leaf or the background. Treat the in-domain PlantVillage score as optimistic; report PlantDoc as the main result. **Change:** a colour-vs-segmented PlantVillage comparison probes background sensitivity. Capture bias stays inside the leaf and may also affect the result (see `background_vs_label_noise.md`). |
| Ahmad, El Gamal, Saraswat (2023). *Toward generalization of deep learning-based plant disease identification under controlled and field conditions.* IEEE Access 11:9042-9057. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3240100 | Do deep learning models for corn disease identification generalise across datasets and imaging conditions? | ImageNet transfer learning with InceptionV3, ResNet50, VGG16, DenseNet169 and Xception (224x224 inputs, 299x299 for InceptionV3; 35/15/50 train/validation/test split) in four experiments. Five corn datasets: PlantVillage, PlantDoc, Digipathos, northern leaf blight (NLB) and their own field dataset CD&S, also used with the background removed (RGBA). | Testing accuracy per class, computed as (TP+TN)/(TP+FP+TN+FN) and averaged over classes; first on held-out images from the training dataset, then on images from the other datasets ("generalization"). | Models trained only on PlantVillage reached up to 99.50% on PlantVillage but at most 45.36% on the other datasets. The highest generalisation accuracy, 81.60% (DenseNet169), came from training on CD&S field images with the background removed. For two diseases only (NLB vs. gray leaf spot), PlantVillage alone generalised at up to 56.20%; adding field images raised this to 79.50% (with PlantDoc), 77.50% (with CD&S) and 80.33% (all three). The authors report watermarks and whole-crop images in PlantDoc. PlantVillage-trained models could not recognise NLB on CD&S images with a black background. Limitation for us: corn only, none of our classes; per-class accuracy that counts true negatives is not comparable with macro-F1. | **Adopt:** background removal as an experimental factor. Their background removal was applied to CD&S field images, not to PlantVillage, so it motivates our colour-vs-segmented PlantVillage comparison but does not predict its result. **Change:** we report macro-F1, not their per-class accuracy. We do not mix PlantDoc into training in the main experiment, because it is our only field test set. Mixing lab and field data stays a possible later experiment with cross-validation on PlantDoc. |
| Selvaraju, Cogswell, Das, Vedantam, Parikh, Batra (2017). *Grad-CAM: Visual explanations from deep networks via gradient-based localization.* ICCV 2017; extended version arXiv:1610.02391 (v4, 2019). https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02391 | How can we see which image regions a CNN's prediction relies on, without changing or retraining the model? | Uses the gradients of the target class flowing into the final convolutional layer to produce a coarse heatmap of the regions important for the prediction; combined with Guided Backpropagation as Guided Grad-CAM. Applied to ImageNet classification models (incl. VGG-16, AlexNet, GoogLeNet, ResNet), image captioning and visual question answering. | Weakly-supervised localization error on ILSVRC-15, comparison with occlusion maps, and human studies of class discrimination and trust. | Better weakly-supervised localization than earlier methods on ILSVRC-15. In a dataset-bias case study, a VGG-16 doctor-vs-nurse classifier reached only 82% test accuracy; Grad-CAM showed it looked at faces and hairstyles. After the training set was rebalanced, test accuracy rose to 90% and the heatmaps moved to white coats and stethoscopes. Limitation for us: the heatmap is coarse (the size of the last convolutional layer) and shows where the model looked, not how much accuracy that costs. | **Adopt:** Grad-CAM on lab and field images to check whether the model looks at the leaf or the background (Week 3 feedback, point 1). **Change:** pair the heatmaps with the colour-vs-segmented PlantVillage comparison, because a heatmap alone does not measure the accuracy drop. |
